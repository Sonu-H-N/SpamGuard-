How SpamGuard Works

A deep dive into Naive Bayes spam classification and the math behind it.

01

Naive Bayes Classifier

SpamGuard uses a Multinomial Naive Bayes classifier — the gold standard for text classification. It's called "naive" because it assumes each word is independent of every other word, which is a simplification, but one that works remarkably well in practice.

Given an email with words w₁, w₂, …, wₙ, the classifier computes:

P(Spam | Email) ∝ P(Spam) × ∏ P(wᵢ | Spam)
P(Ham | Email) ∝ P(Ham) × ∏ P(wᵢ | Ham)

The class with the higher posterior probability wins.

02

Laplace Smoothing

When the model encounters a word it has never seen in training, the raw probability would be zero — which would incorrectly doom any email containing that word. We prevent this with Laplace (add-1) smoothing:

P(wᵢ | class) = (count(wᵢ, class) + α) / (N_class + α × |V|)

Where α = 1 (the smoothing parameter), N_class is total word count in that class, and |V| is vocabulary size. This ensures no word ever has zero probability.

03

Log Probabilities

Multiplying thousands of tiny probabilities together causes numerical underflow — the numbers become so small that floating-point arithmetic loses precision. We solve this by working in log space:

log P(class | Email) = log P(class) + Σ log P(wᵢ | class)

Multiplication becomes addition. The final scores are converted back to probabilities using the softmax function.

04

Training Data

SpamGuard ships with a built-in dataset of — labelled emails derived from the classic SMS Spam Collection and Enron email datasets. The vocabulary covers common spam patterns including:

  • Financial lures (prize, winner, cash, free, offer)
  • Urgency signals (urgent, immediately, expires, act now)
  • Credential phishing (verify, account, password, login)
  • Pharmaceutical spam (pills, medication, prescription)
  • Nigerian advance-fee fraud patterns
  • Legitimate email patterns (meeting, report, attached, regards)

Lowercase

2
Tokenise

3
Remove Stopwords

4
Stem

5
Score

Compute log-probability for each class
